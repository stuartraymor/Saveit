# Try de-essing in Saveit 10.1.6

1. Open a voice recording. Select its clip or track, then click **De-esser** in Audio studio. Use Master effects only if you want it on the whole mix.
2. Click **Open controls** and play a sentence with sharp S or SH sounds. Start with 6000 Hz and 6 dB maximum reduction.
3. Turn on **Listen to S band** briefly. Move Sibilance frequency to find the harshness. This listening aid changes only preview; saved audio always contains the full processed voice.
4. Turn band listening off. Lower Threshold until the Reduction display moves mainly on the harsh sounds. Start with roughly 2–4 dB of reduction; use your ears rather than treating a meter value as a required setting.
5. Compare with Bypass. If the voice becomes dull, lispy, or loses word endings, reduce Maximum reduction or raise Threshold. Release controls how quickly the high frequencies recover.
6. Save a WAV, then listen to the file. Undo removes edits, and effect settings remain editable in the Library.

For a typical tutorial, clean steady noise first, shape EQ, apply gentle compression and de-essing, then match loudness after the final balance. Try the de-esser before or after compression and keep the order that sounds more natural. Put a peak limiter last if you need one; effects or gain after a limiter can raise peaks again.

Each of the 12 effects now has a larger controls window. Older fades and mute use their From/To times; echo adds Delay, Feedback and Wet mix; reverb adds Decay and Wet mix. Compressor and limiter also have reduction displays. The diagrams for echo and reverb are illustrations, not spectrum measurements.

The complete review and recommended next features are in `AUDIO-REVIEW-10.1.6.md`. Test with your normal microphone and voice before publishing important work; the automated tests use controlled audio fixtures rather than a subjective listening panel.
